    About the models

    These are the simplified models of the three schedulers used on the slides and in the video: one core, three jobs, all at nice 0. A step is an event (a job arrives, starts I/O, comes back from I/O or finishes) or a choice the scheduler makes. When only one job is ready and it keeps the CPU, nothing is decided, so that is not a step.

    O(1). A job that sleeps before using up its slice gets the full bonus (115), and a job that uses a whole slice gets the full penalty (125). In the real scheduler the bonus moves gradually, over about a second. The job on the CPU stays at the head of its list; the dashed square shows where it is.

    CFS. A turn is 24 ms divided by the number of runnable jobs (at least 3 ms). A job back from I/O is placed at most 12 ms behind the smallest virtual runtime, and it preempts the running job only if it is more than 4 ms behind it.

    EEVDF. V is the average virtual runtime of the runnable jobs, and a job is eligible if it is not ahead of V. Each turn is 3 ms. New jobs and jobs back from I/O are placed at V, with no credit. When two eligible jobs have the same deadline, the one that was queued first runs.

    Time advances in steps of 0.1 ms, so a few switches happen 0.1 ms after the event that causes them (CFS at 20.1 ms, for example).
