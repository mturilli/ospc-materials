    About the model

    The partition. 8 CPUs and nothing else. Our cluster's partitions have two nodes with 512 CPUs each, and memory and GPUs count too, but the rules are the same.

    The order of the queue. Here it is the order in which jobs arrive. On our cluster Slurm also counts fair share: users who ran a lot recently move down the queue. sprio shows a pending job's priority.

    Slurm plans with time limits. It never knows how long a job will really run, so it assumes every job runs until its time limit. A job that reaches its limit is stopped (state TIMEOUT), so ask for the time you expect plus a margin. Without --time, a job gets the partition's default: 4 hours on cpu and gpu, 2 hours on interactive.

    Reservations and backfill. The first job in the queue that does not fit waits with reason Resources, and Slurm reserves CPUs for it at the earliest time they will be free. A job further down starts early only if it cannot delay that reservation; the others wait with reason Priority. squeue --start shows the start time Slurm expects for your pending jobs. Slurm repeats this every 30 seconds and can hold a reservation for many jobs at once; with one blocked job at a time, as here, it makes the same choices.

    The table under the chart uses squeue's words: ST is the state (PD pending, R running), TIME the time the job has run so far, and REASON why a pending job waits.
