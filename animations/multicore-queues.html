    About the model

    These are the two designs of OSTEP chapter 10, in a small model that gives the schedules of the video. Time is counted in ticks, one cell of the timeline. A step is a tick where something is decided: a slice ends, a job finishes, or a core steals a job. Switching jobs takes no time, and the model does not simulate caches: a move to another core only means that the job starts there with a cold cache.

    One queue. Each slice is 1 tick. When the slices end, the four jobs go back to the end of the shared queue in core order (core 0 first), then cores 0 to 3 each take the first job in the queue. On a real system the cores would also wait for each other on the queue's lock.

    One queue per core. Each core runs round robin with slices of 2 ticks. C needs 4 ticks of CPU; A, B and D run past the end. Stealing starts at tick 12, so you can first see the imbalance. From then on, every 4 ticks, the core with fewer jobs takes the first job waiting on the other core and adds it to the back of its own queue. Real schedulers, like Linux's, check more often and look at the load and at the caches before they move a job.

    In the video, from tick 12 the cores switch jobs every tick, as in the OSTEP figure. Here the slices stay at 2 ticks, so the schedule after tick 12 is a little different: the same jobs move at the same ticks, and A, B and D still get 8 ticks each.
