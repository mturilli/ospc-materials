    About the measurements and the models

    Where. julia, a node of our cluster, 11 November 2026: NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition (188 SMs, 96 GB). A job with 4 CPUs and one GPU, CuPy 14.2. GPU times are medians from cupyx.profiler.benchmark; NumPy times are the best of 5 runs, one thread. haskell's RTX 6000 Ada (142 SMs, 48 GB) gives the same shapes, about 1.8 times slower on large arrays.

    Warps. A model with two numbers: 1 turn of work and 8 turns of waiting per instruction, one scheduler that takes the warp that has been ready longest. A real SM has 4 schedulers, up to 48 warps, and waits several hundred cycles for its memory.

    Launches. Measured: 4.6 µs of CPU time per launch (20,000 launches without waiting) and 0.81 ms for a + b on 1e8 float32. Schematic: 2 µs of GPU work for 10,000 numbers and 1 µs from a launch to the start of its kernel.

    Size. Measured, float32, a + b with both arrays already on the GPU. Between 1 and 10 million numbers the three arrays fit in the GPU's L2 cache, so the rate passes 2 TB/s; from 100 million on it is the memory's 1.48 TB/s.

    Fusion. Measured: 3.81 ms for the six kernels, 1.07 ms for cp.fuse and for an ElementwiseKernel, 176 ms for NumPy. Bytes counted from the arrays each kernel reads and writes (x * x reads x once).
