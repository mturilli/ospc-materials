    About the measurements

    Where. haskell, a node of our cluster (AMD EPYC 9755), one batch job with 64 CPUs (32 cores), 7 November 2026. Every time is the median of 5 runs; the runs differ by a few percent.

    The program. One worker builds and sorts 40 million order values (about 1 s). Then a ProcessPoolExecutor with n workers runs the tasks. Compute tasks draw random numbers on small arrays that fit in the core's cache (640 tasks). Data tasks each average a million orders picked at random from the 320 MB array (1280 tasks). The same program is in the notebook of Session 23.

    Speedup is the time with one worker divided by the time with n workers; efficiency is the speedup divided by n. Amdahl's law uses s = the prepare time divided by the total time with one worker.

    Weak scaling gives every worker 20 compute tasks (or 40 data tasks), so the problem grows with the workers. The chart shows the pool time; the prepare step does not grow.
