    About the model

    This is the multi-level feedback queue used on the slides and in the video: three queues, Q2 (high) to Q0 (low), and time in steps of 1 ms. A job may use 10 ms of CPU in a queue before it moves down (Rule 4); in Q0 it simply goes to the back of the queue every 10 ms. Jobs in the same queue share the CPU in round robin. A step is an event (a job arrives, moves down, starts or ends I/O, or finishes, or a boost) or a choice the scheduler makes. When only one job is ready and it keeps the CPU, nothing is decided, so that is not a step.

    Rules 1–4. A needs 1,000 ms of CPU and arrives at 0. B needs 15 ms and arrives at 45 ms. C arrives at 70 ms and does 2 ms of CPU and 3 ms of I/O, four times. The run stops at 100 ms, long before A is done. The dashed boxes show when A is ready but waiting.

    Rule 5. A is a long job that has already moved down to Q0. A new request of 10 ms arrives every 10 ms, so Q2 always has a job. With the boost, every 50 ms all jobs move back to Q2, behind the jobs already there.

    Switching jobs takes no time here, and a boost or a new job takes effect at the start of a millisecond.
