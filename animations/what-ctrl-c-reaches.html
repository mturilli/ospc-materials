    About this page

    What was checked. An interactive bash in a real terminal ran a copy of stats.sh with 4 workers (Ubuntu 26.04, bash 5.3, 9 November 2026). Before Ctrl-C, the script's bash was a process group of its own and the terminal's foreground group, and the workers were in it. The workers' SigIgn had SIGINT set; the script's bash did not. After Ctrl-C, and also after kill -INT on the script's bash alone, the script's bash was gone, the workers were still running with PPID 1, and the shell printed 130. With the two traps, nothing was left.

    PID 1. In the course container PID 1 is the login shell, bash -l, the first process of the container. On an ordinary Linux machine PID 1 is the init process (systemd), and the orphans go to it, or to a process that asked to adopt orphans. The PIDs 805 and 812-815 are those of the review question.

    Job control. An interactive shell gives each job its own process group and can bring a job to the foreground. A script runs without job control: the commands it starts with & stay in its group, with SIGINT and SIGQUIT ignored, so that Ctrl-C does not stop background work by accident.
