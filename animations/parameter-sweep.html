    About the model

    An illustrative model, not a measurement. The job has 32 CPUs, and each CPU runs one thread at a time. The sweep is 64 independent fits. With 1 thread, a fit takes 1s: 2,000 products of small matrices, 0.5ms each. Every time on this page comes from this model.

    Level 1, workers. Each of the worker processes runs one fit at a time, so with W workers the 64 fits take 64 ÷ W rounds.

    Level 2, BLAS threads. Inside a fit, BLAS splits each product among the worker's threads. In the model, a quarter of each small product does not split: handing out the parts and waiting for the last one. So a fit gains at most 4 times, however many threads it gets (Amdahl's law, Lecture 16): 1.6 times with 2 threads, 2.3 with 4, 2.9 with 8, 3.4 with 16 and 3.7 with 32. Meanwhile, the other threads wait (hatched).

    More threads than CPUs. When workers × threads is more than 32, the threads take turns in time slices of 5ms. A thread that has finished its part keeps its CPU until its slice ends, so each part of each product costs a whole slice: the model of Threads Inside the Libraries, with the same 5ms slice. With T threads per worker, the sweep then takes 64 fits × 2,000 products × T parts × 5ms ÷ 32 CPUs = 20s × T.

    Measured (Lecture 17 and Threads Inside the Libraries). In a job with 4 CPUs, 16 tasks of small products: 4 workers × 1 thread took 1.41s; 4 workers × 4 threads took 6s with 500 × 500 products and 88.5s with 200 × 200. With 16 CPUs, 16 workers × 16 threads took 664s, against 1.6s with 1 thread each. The model's option C, 640s against 2s for A, is a collapse of the same order.

    Why A. The 64 fits are independent, so the sweep is already parallel at the level of the fits: 32 workers keep the 32 CPUs working, with nothing to wait for. BLAS threads would split small products that gain little, and more threads than CPUs make every product wait for turns. Parallelize one level and set the other to 1 (Lecture 17): here, 32 workers and OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 in the job script. joblib with n_jobs=32 does the same on its own: each worker gets 32 ÷ 32 = 1 thread. One large product is different: there the library's threads pay off (Threads Inside the Libraries, view One product).
