    About the measurements

    Where. haskell, a node of our cluster (AMD EPYC 9755), one core, float64, in a 4-CPU batch job. Every time is the best of 7 runs.

    The Python loop is for i in range(n): c[i] = a[i] + b[i] on two lists of a million numbers: 26 ns per number (on an Apple laptop, in the course container: about 18 ns). The five boxes on the page are a simplified picture of what the interpreter does for each number.

    The compiled loops are one C loop, c[i] = a[i] + b[i], compiled three ways with gcc 14: without vectorization (one number per instruction), and with 256-bit (AVX2, 4 doubles) or 512-bit (AVX-512, 8 doubles) vector instructions. NumPy's own loops use the same instructions. The multiply-add version evaluates a polynomial of degree 8 on each number.

    In cache or from RAM. In cache: 4,096 numbers, processed again and again, so they stay in the core's caches. From RAM: 10 million numbers (80 MB per array).

    Apple laptops have 128-bit vector registers (NEON): 2 doubles per instruction, but several instructions per cycle. Exercise 2 measures the Python loop and NumPy on your own laptop.
