    About the model

    These are the schedules used on the slides and in the video. Three jobs arrive together at t = 0, in the order A, B, C. A needs 100 s of CPU, B and C need 10 s each, and none of them does I/O. A step is a moment when a scheduler picks a job: a job starts, finishes, or its time slice ends. When only one job is left and it keeps running, nothing is decided, so that is not a step.

    All jobs arrive at 0, so the response time of a job is the time of its first run, and its turnaround time is the time it is done. Both columns on the right show them as they happen.

    SJF. B and C need the same time, so B runs first because it is first in line. SJF knows how long each job will run, which a real OS does not.

    Round Robin. Each slice is 1 s. A job whose slice is over goes to the back of the queue. Here a switch between jobs takes no time; on a real system each switch costs some time, as the slide on time slices shows.
